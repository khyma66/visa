# U.S. legal and policy launch review

Research checked September 11, 2026 UTC. **Issue-spotting checklist, not legal advice, an exhaustive survey of every U.S. law, or a compliance certification.** Federal, state and local obligations depend on the operator, users, data, age range, monetization and actual conduct. A U.S. attorney should determine applicability and approve final policies. U.S. hosting does not exclude foreign obligations when targeting or serving people abroad.

## Decisions needed from the owner

1. Legal operator name, business/individual status, operating state, service address, public domain and monitored support/privacy/abuse contacts.
2. Audience and age policy. Recommended initial scope: adults 18+, text-only peer Q&A and messaging, no advertising/payments and no third-party imports in production until separately approved. This is a recommendation, not an adopted or enforced policy yet.
3. States/countries served and whether the service will intentionally target users outside the U.S.; data locations, processors and contractual protections.
4. Which data is necessary, its retention periods, deletion/export handling, incident/legal-preservation exceptions and who handles requests.
5. Whether imported content is licensed/otherwise lawfully reusable; whether any legal professionals, paid referrals, sponsored recommendations or AI eligibility advice will be offered.

## Applicability and implementation matrix

| Topic | Why it matters here | Required decision/work before launch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FTC privacy/security and truthful claims | Email, messages and sensitive visa experiences are processed. Promises about anonymity or security must match reality. | Minimize data; restrict access; implement reasonable security; accurately describe providers, logging, message access and tracking. Never promise “unhackable,” guaranteed anonymity or professional verification. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/start-security-guide-business). |
| CalOPPA privacy policy | California’s rule can apply to commercial websites collecting personal information from California consumers, independent of the larger CCPA business thresholds. | Publish a conspicuous, accurate policy covering collection, sharing, changes and tracking disclosures. The current preview notice explicitly has unresolved operator/contact/retention details and is not the final policy. [California AG](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/facts/online-privacy/privacy-policy). |
| CCPA/CPRA, if applicable | Assess doing business in California and the relevant thresholds: current CPPA FAQ lists $26.625m gross annual revenue, buying/selling/sharing 100,000 consumers’/households’ information, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing it. A million accounts alone does not automatically establish the buying/selling/sharing threshold. | Counsel assesses applicability and definitions, not just revenue. If covered, implement notices at collection, verified access/correction/deletion requests, opt-outs/recognized signals where applicable, sensitive-data restrictions, contracts and non-discrimination. Evaluate the 2026 risk-assessment/other regulatory changes using their actual triggers and phased dates. [CPPA FAQ](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq), [2026 regulations update](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html). |
| Other state privacy and breach laws | Users’ locations matter, not just the operator’s state. Exemptions, sensitive-data definitions, request/appeal rights and breach rules differ. | Maintain a state-by-state applicability register with counsel. Do not apply California thresholds everywhere; Texas, for example, generally exempts SBA small businesses but retains a sensitive-data-sale consent rule. Define jurisdiction-specific notification and rights procedures. [Texas AG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/es/node/259071), [FTC breach response](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/data-breach-response-guide-business). |
| COPPA and minors | Under-13 users and child-directed services have specific federal rules; teen requirements can also arise under state laws. A warning or checkbox is not blanket immunity. | Finalize age eligibility, implement a proportionate age/knowledge response and handle discovered underage accounts. If children are admitted or COPPA applies, implement required consent, notice, security/retention and parental rights under the amended rule. Do not collect birthdates or identity documents unnecessarily. [FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). |
| Immigration advice and scams | Individualized legal advice/representation and professional advertising can invoke authorization and state professional rules. | Position the product as peer experiences; never imply USCIS affiliation. Link official guidance, explain accepted answers are not professional verification, and prohibit guaranteed-approval scams. Independently verify any promoted attorney/accredited representative before presenting them as qualified. A disclaimer does not cure unauthorized practice. [USCIS authorized-help brochure](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/brochures/UPIL%20Brochure_English.pdf). |
| Copyright, DMCA and imported content | Posts/comments may be protected works or contain personal information. Paying Apify or adding attribution does not grant republication rights. Source-platform contractual restrictions are separate. | Review source terms, permissions/lawful basis and individual content rights. For applicable section 512 safe-harbor protection, counsel should implement the conditions, including designated-agent registration/public details, notice/counter-notice process and repeat-infringer policy. Safe harbor is conditional and not a license for the operator’s own copying. Add durable takedown suppression across imports, caches and refreshes. [Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/512/), [Facebook scraping explanation](https://www.facebook.com/help/463983701520800). |
| TAKE IT DOWN Act | Covered platforms, including relevant social/messaging services, must now have a qualifying removal process; the deadline was May 19, 2026. Text-only design does not justify assuming the service is exempt without assessing its functions. | Counsel confirms coverage. Provide conspicuous intake accessible to nonmembers and victims/authorized representatives, required request fields, protected handling and staffed escalation. Covered platforms must remove covered intimate images and known identical copies within 48 hours of a valid request. Existing signed-in reports are not a substitute. [FTC enforcement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act). |
| Child exploitation reporting/preservation | Relevant providers can have duties when they obtain actual knowledge of apparent covered violations. | Counsel and trained trust-and-safety staff establish CyberTipline reporting and statutory preservation processes under 18 U.S.C. §2258A. Do not ask ordinary testers to find, download or send abuse material. Use harmless fixtures. A community ban does not eliminate provider duties. [Current U.S. Code](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A2258A+edition%3Aprelim%29). |
| Accessibility | Public-accommodation accessibility obligations and state law need assessment; DOJ distinguishes business guidance from government-specific rules. | Test keyboard navigation, labels, focus, screen readers, contrast, zoom, mobile layouts and accessible complaints. Use WCAG 2.2 AA as an engineering target, not a claim that it is the identical legal standard for every private business. [DOJ guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/). |
| Commercial email and endorsements | Signup/recovery mail is not automatically marketing. Sponsored services or referral recommendations can create disclosure obligations. | Keep security email separate. Before marketing, implement applicable CAN-SPAM sender/address/opt-out rules and honor opt-outs; review mixed transactional/promotional messages. Require clear material-connection disclosures for paid recommendations and prohibit fake reviews. [FTC CAN-SPAM](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), [FTC endorsements](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews). |

## Additional counsel review before expanding

Assess enforceable terms and consent/version records, content display license, complaints/appeals, lawful law-enforcement requests, state wiretap/session-replay restrictions before analytics, data-broker registration if the business model fits, and subscription/refund/tax/payment requirements if monetization begins. Section 230 is not blanket immunity from copyright, privacy, criminal or the operator’s own conduct; do not rely on it as a substitute for this review. No claim of HIPAA, GDPR or other certification should be made without a specific applicability assessment and implementation.

## Notices added to the app

- Signup: public pseudonym vs identity, provider-held email, preview/privacy/rules links; no fictional consent to final terms or false age verification.
- Ask and answer forms: public/indexable/copyable content; remove identifying documents; share only permitted text.
- Questions: official-source verification, no government affiliation, no guarantees; imports are incomplete snapshots and source links can identify people.
- Messages: no end-to-end encryption; recipients may copy messages; reported-message and administrator access; no documents/codes/money for guaranteed outcomes.
- Footer and `/community-safety`: official resources and policy links.
- `/privacy`, `/terms`, `/contact`: accurate preview notices and explicit missing-policy/intake limitations. **These must be finalized and made operational before public launch.**

No operator identity, contact address, age eligibility, retention promise, liability waiver or DMCA-agent status was invented. The release checklist now requires specific legal, age, request-processing and takedown evidence; passing a checklist is still not a legal opinion.
